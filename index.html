Small actions, big difference

What is SDG 12?

SDG 12 is one of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals set by the United Nations. It aims to reduce waste, use natural resources wisely and make production and shopping less harmful to the environment.

Where do the problems start?

Overconsumption, mass production and wrong waste handling lead to wasted food, wasted products and wasted energy and water. Much of what we throw away could still be used.

Why our choices matter

Food does not have to go to waste

About one third of the food produced in the world is lost or wasted, while many people still go hungry. Wasted food also wastes the water, land and energy used to grow, transport and store it. Planning meals, storing food well and using leftovers are simple ways to help.

Things should last longer

Every product we buy needs materials, water and energy to make. When we repair, borrow, swap or buy second-hand, we use fewer resources and create less waste. Recycling is important, but the best waste is the waste we never create.

The goal for 2030

The United Nations wants countries to halve food waste per person in shops and homes by 2030 and to greatly reduce waste through prevention, reuse and recycling. Companies are asked to produce in cleaner ways, and people to think before they buy. Big changes are made of many small decisions, including ours.

Conclusion

Responsible choices start with small steps. If we plan what we buy, repair and reuse things, sort our waste and throw away less food, we protect water, energy and nature. SDG 12 is not about giving things up. It is about making smarter choices, one day at a time. Start with one habit from our poll today.
